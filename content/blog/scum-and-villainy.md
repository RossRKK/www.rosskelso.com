Recently I convinced my starfinder group to indulge me and try out playing "Scum
and Villainy" (the sci-fi hack of Blades in the Dark). I had recently been
thinking that while I enjoy playing starfinder I often find the combat can drag,
with each round taking a significant length of time and each player only getting
to take 3 actions.

It has always been true that in games I've ran (and played) the most fun is had
in exploration mode where the players and the GM have freedom to to essentially
just mess around. Recently I had the oppurtunity to introduce some new players
to starfinder which resulted in a lot of fun setting up the stakes but the same
seemed to drag once initiative rolled.

To a not insignifcant degree this can be attributed to the players not being
that familiar with the game. In fact this also goes for my main group to some
extent as due to the style of play and my use of milestone levelling the number
of combats per level was actually very low. Combine that with being very
tolerant of absences and it means that a player may not have time to learn their
new combat abilities.

Furthermore, my group started playing starfinder 2e at level 4 after switching
from starfinder 1e when the playtest first began.

By trying scum and villainy I had hoped to allow the fun setup part of the game
to become the entire game and therefore make the game more fun. However what we
found during our playtest was that it didn't really click.

I found constantly having to come up with consequences for partial failures
pretty difficult (I suspect that I would get better at this over time). I think
the biggest issue though was with the system of position and effect. Choosing
position and effect often felt arbitrary, and at least from where I was sitting
it felt like the players had the ability to really stretch plausibility in the
narrative when stating actions.

One of my players when I asked for opinions after the session said it felt like
there weren't any stakes to what was happening. Like it was just s sequence of
unrelated events happening after each other. I think that this is largely due to
our unfamilairity with the system constantly getting in the way of our ability
to lean into it.

When playing starfinder I find it very natural to set DCs in response to player
actions and lean into player role play choices. If a player ever wants to do
something implausible, I don't have to say that will have limited (or perhaps
even no effect) I can use the tools at my disposal to just set a very high DC.
If they meet it then we just roll with it.

I have in the past gone out of my way to remove some of the mechanical crunch
from starfinder. When presented with second edition and reading the GM Core
section on subsystem I decided to come up with my own narrative form of starship
combat which is not in small part inspired by the mechanics of forged in the
dark systems. TODO: claude add the cross link to the relevant article.

I think going forward we will stick to starfinder. The universe we've built
together is vibrant and the skill mechanics are familiar - even if the combat
ones are less so. I hope that as my main group continues our new campaign (now
reset back to level 1 and we get the opportunity to learn how our characters
work that combat will become less of a slog and more of a tight tactical game.
The kind of thing I really enjoy in Baldur's Gate 3 (which has the significant
advantage of using a computer to resolve all game actions very quickly) and
