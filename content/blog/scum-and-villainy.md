Recently I convinced my starfinder group to indulge me and try out playing
[Scum and Villainy](https://offguardgames.com/scum-and-villainy/) (the sci-fi
hack of [Blades in the Dark](https://bladesinthedark.com/)). I had recently been
thinking that while I enjoy playing starfinder I often find the combat can drag,
with each round taking a significant length of time and each player only getting
to take 3 actions.

It has always been true that in games I've run (and played) the most fun is had
in exploration mode where the players and the GM have freedom to essentially
just mess around. Recently I had the opportunity to introduce some new players
to starfinder which resulted in a lot of fun setting up the stakes but it seemed
to drag once initiative rolled.

To a not insignificant degree this can be attributed to the players not being
that familiar with the game. In fact this also goes for my main group to some
extent as due to the style of play and my use of milestone levelling the number
of combats per level has been very low. Combine that with being very tolerant of
absences and it means that a player may not have time to learn their new combat
abilities. Furthermore, my group started playing starfinder 2e at level 4 after
switching from starfinder 1e when the playtest first began.

By trying scum and villainy I had hoped to allow the fun setup part of the game
to become the entire game. However what we found during our playtest was that it
didn't really click.

## Initial Impressions

I found constantly having to come up with consequences for partial failures
pretty difficult (I suspect that I would get better at this over time). I think
the biggest issue though was with the system of position and effect. Choosing
position and effect often felt arbitrary, and at least from where I was sitting
it felt like the players had the ability to really stretch plausibility in the
narrative when stating actions. This is likely inexperience preventing me from
being able to state position and effect effectively.

One of my players when I asked for opinions after the session said it felt like
there weren't any stakes to what was happening. Like it was just a sequence of
unrelated events happening after each other. I think that this is largely due to
our unfamiliarity with the system constantly getting in the way of our ability
to lean into it.

## DCs and Degrees of Success

When playing starfinder I find it very natural to set DCs in response to player
actions and lean into player role play choices. If a player ever wants to do
something implausible, I don't have to say that will have limited (or perhaps
even no) effect - I can use the tools at my disposal to just set a very high DC.

Scum and Villainy suffers from an issue which other games like Call of Cthulhu
suffer from: where the GM doesn't have control of setting how difficult an
action is. In the case of scum and villainy only how effective it is and how
severe the consequences of failure are (through position). I think I prefer
systems where there is a DC to beat which the GM can set arbitrarily high if
they'd like to, without directly negating the player's ability to take the
action if it is "impossible" or "very unlikely to work" - as is probably more
common. In the rules for Scum and Villainy there is an example of an action with
"no effect" where a player tries to shoot a fleeing starship with their pistol
[^no-effect-example]. In this example the GM basically has to shut down this
option because otherwise any roll higher than a 4 on a d6 would cause this
starship to be stopped by a single character shooting it with a pistol. The
rules encourage everyone to understand why there is a no effect ruling here and
come up with new actions to get around it (like getting a bigger gun). But if
there is time pressure that makes that not work in the fiction then the answer
is essentially "no".

Conversely in a game like starfinder if a player wants to do something that's
very unlikely to work I can still let them. I can still have their character do
what the player wants them to do. I can let them know that this is very unlikely
to work and set the DC very high. Then on the off chance they roll high enough
they can still succeed. In pathfinder/starfinder 2e especially I can set the DC
so high that even on a 20 they only succeed because on a nat 20 you go up a
degree of success, and give them the standard effect instead of an extra
critical effect.

## Planning

One of the core features of forged in the dark games is the fact that the
players skip the planning and use flashbacks to retroactively come up with a
plan.

In part I think this was part of what was disorienting for my players in our
play test session: the pre-given scenario puts them in a situation the players
did not prepare for and so they have less concept of what is going on.

More fundamentally though I think this is a problem simply because a lot of
where my table gets the fun from is from coming up with a truly ludicrous
quarter baked plan, and then watching it go wrong. You lose some of the fun of
that when the plan isn't yours. This issue may in part be remedied by not using
a starting scenario from the book and instead starting in a more player led way.

## Combat Crunch

Being able to resolve "combat" in scum and villainy so quickly was legitimately
very cool. But I do wonder if me giving the players too much ability to affect
the narrative by not limiting effect more caused some of the loss of stakes. In
general I allowed them to incapacitate those in their way in 1 or 2 checks
(mainly because the players chose the action and that's what they asked to do).
However, I think the game would really suffer if I introduced some kind of
pseudo-hit point system by forcing players to take multiple rounds to down an
enemy (I guess I'm describing setting up a clock).

I have in the past gone out of my way to remove some of the mechanical crunch
from starfinder. When presented with second edition and reading the GM Core
section on subsystem I decided to come up with my own narrative form of starship
combat which is not in small part inspired by the mechanics of forged in the
dark systems. You can read about it in
[Starfinder 2e - Star Ship Encounter Sub-system](@/blog/starship-encounter.md).
I created this both due to the lack of starship combat in starfinder 2e at the
time and explicitly in response to issues we had previously found with
starfinder 1e's starship combat system.

## Conclusion

I'm glad I took the time to play scum and villainy. However I don't think it was
the solution I was looking for. I also admit that a lot of my criticisms of this
game come from me running it badly due to a lack of familiarity. But I guess in
some sense that was the point. Every time I have changed system (5e ->
starfinder 1e -> starfinder 2e) I have always done a test session first. The
similarity of these systems to each other allowed that to work relatively
smoothly (it is worth noting that prior to playing 5e I played a lot of BG3).

I think that if I decided to spend the time becoming familiar with this system
it might turn out to work well for my group. But on the other hand: I think that
we're comfortable in starfinder 2e, and I think that the pathfinder/starfinder
2e system better supports me with the tools I want to tweak the game with
sub-systems that will work with my group.

As we go forward I will likely spend some time streamlining starfinder combat. I
have plenty of ideas. Like running more combats with fewer high level monsters
instead of many low level monsters (less time on the GMs turn), pre-preparing
finite state machines for enemy behaviour so that monster moves are more
mechanical.

I hope that as my main group continues our new campaign (now reset back to
level 1) and we get the opportunity to learn how our characters work, that
combat will become less of a slog and more of a tight tactical game. The kind of
thing I really enjoy in Baldur's Gate 3 (which has the significant advantage of
using a computer to resolve all game actions very quickly) and board games like
[Undaunted Normandy](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/268864/undaunted-normandy).
